I walk the same two miles every evening. For a year I wrote down one thing from each walk, and the list taught me more about noticing than about walking.
{{< /lead >}}

The route never changes: down the hill, along the water, back up past the bakery that closes at six. What changes is what I see, and for the first few weeks, I saw almost nothing.

## What a year of notes looks like

Most entries are a single line. A heron on the third post. The bakery's light still on at seven. A [neighbor's new gate](#), painted the wrong green.

{{< quote size="large" cite="From the notebook, March" >}}
The walk is the same; the walker isn't.
{{< /quote >}}

{{< callout kind="tip" >}}
Write the note before you get home. By the front door, it has already turned into a story.
{{< /callout >}}

## What I would do differently

Keep the notebook, and keep a second list of the walks I skipped, with the reason. The reasons turned out to be the more honest record.

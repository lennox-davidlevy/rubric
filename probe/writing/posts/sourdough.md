---
title: My first sourdough that actually rose
---

I have tried to make sourdough bread six times this year. Five of those loaves could have been used as doorstops. This weekend, finally, one rose, and I want to write down exactly what I did before I forget.

The starter came from my neighbor Ruth, who has kept hers alive since 1998. She told me the secret is patience, which is not something I am known for.

> "Bread will tell you when it's ready. Your clock won't."

Here is what you need:

- 500 g bread flour
- 350 g water, lukewarm
- 100 g active starter
- 10 g salt

1. Mix the flour and water and leave it for an hour.
2. Add the starter and the salt, and squeeze it all through your fingers until it comes together.
3. Every half hour for two hours, stretch the dough and fold it over itself.
4. Leave it covered on the counter until it has grown by about half. Mine took six hours.
5. Shape it into a ball, put it in a floured bowl, and leave it in the fridge overnight.
6. Bake in a very hot covered pot for 20 minutes, then uncovered for 25 more.

Be careful taking the lid off the pot. The steam that comes out will burn you, and I have the mark on my wrist to prove it.

The crust crackled while it cooled, which Ruth says is the sound of a good loaf. The inside had big uneven holes, the kind you see in bakery windows.

Next time I want to try adding whole wheat flour. If you have a starter and an afternoon, give it a go.

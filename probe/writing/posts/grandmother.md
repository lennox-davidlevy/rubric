---
title: What my grandmother kept
---

When my grandmother died, she left a house full of things nobody wanted and one shoebox everybody fought over.

The shoebox held letters. Not from my grandfather, as we all assumed, but from a woman named Helen who had lived across the street from her in 1952. They wrote to each other for forty years after Helen moved away, and my grandmother never once mentioned her.

I read them over a weekend, in order. The early ones are about recipes and husbands and the price of shoes. By the 1970s they are about something else entirely, though neither of them ever says what.

> I keep your letters in the box with the good scissors, where nobody looks.

That line is from my grandmother, in a draft she never sent. It was folded inside the last letter from Helen, dated 1993.

My mother thinks we should burn them. My aunt thinks we should give them to a library. I think the box was the point: she kept them where nobody looked, and for thirty years, nobody did.

Some things are not secrets. They are just private, and the difference matters more than I understood before that weekend.

I put the box back on the shelf in my closet. I haven't decided anything yet.

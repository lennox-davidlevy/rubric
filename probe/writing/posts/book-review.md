---
title: The Lighthouse Year, reviewed
---

I picked up The Lighthouse Year because of the cover and kept reading because of the first sentence, which is one of the best I've read in a while.

> The keeper's wife counted the ships the way other people count sheep, and she slept about as well.

The novel follows a family who take over a lighthouse on a small island in 1911. The father keeps the light, the mother keeps the accounts, and their daughter, who narrates, keeps everything else, including several secrets that aren't hers.

The writing is spare and very precise. Nothing is described twice, and the island itself becomes a character: you learn its paths and its weather the way the daughter does, slowly, by walking them.

Where it falls down is the last third. A storm arrives, as storms in lighthouse novels must, and the plot speeds up in a way the rest of the book never needed. Two characters make choices I didn't believe for a second.

Still, I'd recommend it, especially for a long weekend somewhere with bad weather of your own.

My rating: four out of five.

If you liked this, try The Salt Path, or anything by Sarah Moss.
